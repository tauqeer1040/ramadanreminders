## What "best" means for a Muslim journal (5 tests)

We studied Saraly, Muhasaba, Niyyah, and the general “best” lists (Day One, Journey). A journal earns the word *best* for a Muslim if it passes these five:

### 1. It gives you *your* verse, not a random one

Most Islamic journals give a daily verse on a schedule. Meowmin reverses it: **write your day, get the verse that matches what you lived.** Lost your temper in traffic → `“…and when they are angry, they forgive.” — 42:37`. Checked your bank balance at bedtime → `“…and whoever puts their trust in Allah, He will be enough for them.” — 65:3`. No random aayahs.

[Try it → Open Meowmin](/app) and scratch your first three cards — each is a verse matched to you.

### 2. The journal *is* dhikr — not a dhikr counter you manage

Meowmin is built as the best journal for remembering Allah *through your own life*. You don’t open a separate tasbih and count to 33. You write. The reflection *is* remembrance. As you read the verse that fits your day, dhikr happens naturally. Mindfulness is the feature — not another counter to maintain.

> *Not a fatwa source.* Like Saraly’s humble phrasing, Meowmin invites reflection with a verse. It never issues rulings. For fiqh, ask a scholar you trust.

### 3. Streak that survives real life

Every app loves streaks. Meowmin loves *you keeping it*. Streaks with **shields** — 3 with Starter, 18 with the 4-Month Challenge (114 Surahs · 120 Days), 72 with Yearly (30 Juz · 12 Months), 150 shared across 3 users with Lifetime. Miss a day on a hectic day? A shield holds your streak. No guilt leaderboard.

### 4. Private by design, not by fine print

Your journal is encrypted and yours alone — never used to train models, never sold, never viewed. It stays between you and Allah. You can export your words anytime.

### 5. Ramadan-ready without tracking fasts

Ramadan isn’t about logging fasts in another tracker. It’s about being a more mindful Muslim. Meowmin was made for this: by writing a little each day *before* Ramadan, you arrive already carrying the Quran with you — more present, more connected, without adding another habit to manage.

## Quick Good Journaling: 280 Characters That Beat a Blank Page

The winners we studied (Quran In Depth, The Holy Quran) ask for 10 minutes + 6 fields (Reference / Theme / Observation / Question / Dua / Action). Beautiful — and heavy.

Meowmin’s **2-Minute Good Journal** is lighter *and* more likely to survive:

1.  **One line** — “My daughter held my hand crossing the street today.”
2.  **One verse** — `“If you are grateful, I will surely increase you.” — 14:7`
3.  **One action** — Text her tonight: “Love you.”

280 characters is enough. It keeps the habit loop intact. Keep your journal on your mushaf (“Always Open” rule from the 2025 prompts guide) and you’re 3× more likely to use it.

*If you’re exhausted:* Your entry can be one word. “Sabr.” “Shukr.” That still counts. That still saves your streak.
